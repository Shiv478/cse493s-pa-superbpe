Project  Proposal:  Parity-Aware  SuperBPE  Fair  Superword  Tokenization  for  Multilingual  Language  
Models
 
Goals  and  Hypotheses  
Recent  developments  in  superword  tokenization,  such  as  SuperBPE  [1]  and  BoundlessBPE  [2],  remove  
the
 
constraint
 
that
 
tokens
 
must
 
remain
 
within
 
whitespace-separated
 
spans.
 
These
 
methods
 
outperform
 
Byte
 
Pair
 
Encoding
 
(BPE),
 
with
 
reported
 
gains
 
of
 
+4.0%
 
across
 
30
 
tasks
 
on
 
an
 
8B-scale
 
model
 
and
 
27%
 
lower
 
inference
 
compute.
 
However,
 
most
 
evaluations
 
are
 
English-only.
 
The
 
work
 
in
 
sources
 
3
 
and
 
4
  
also
 
show
 
that
 
tokenizers
 
can
 
introduce
 
substantial
 
cross-lingual
 
bias,
 
with
 
disparities
 
of
 
up
 
to
 
15
 
times
 
across
 
languages.
 
Because
 
SuperBPE’s
 
second
 
stage
 
merges
 
across
 
whitespace-separated
 
spans,
 
we
 
conjecture
 
that
 
it
 
may
 
favor
 
English-like
 
languages
 
and
 
disadvantage
 
languages
 
with
 
different
 
writing
 
systems
 
or
 
boundary
 
structures,
 
such
 
as
 
Chinese.
 
Due  to  this  we  want  to  build  Parity-Aware  SuperBPE  (PA-SuperBPE) ,  which  integrates  the  fairness  
idea
 
from
 
Parity-Aware
 
BPE
 
from
 
source
 
5
 
into
 
SuperBPE’s
 
two-phase
 
curriculum.
 
The
 
key
 
change
 
is
 
a
 
parity-aware
 
merging
 
step
 
that
 
prioritizes
 
compression
 
gains
 
for
 
the
 
currently
 
most
 
poorly
 
compressed
 
language.
 
Hypotheses:  
Hypothesis  1 :  
The  unmodified  SuperBPE  increases  cross-linguistic  disparities  compared  to  BPE  when  vocabulary  sizes  
are
 
comparable,
 
using
 
the
 
Gini
 
index
 
on
 
tokens
 
per
 
character
 
per
 
language;
 
 
Hypothesis  2:  
The  PA-SuperBPE  maintains  a  significant  amount  of  the  improvements  achieved  by  SuperBPE  on  
English
 
while
 
minimizing
 
cross-linguistic
 
disparities
 
compared
 
to
 
both
 
SuperBPE
 
and
 
BPE;
 
 
Hypothesis  3:   
Fairness-aware  tokenization  may  improve  performance  for  underrepresented  languages  in  small  
pretrained
 
language
 
models
 
on
 
FLORES-200
 
perplexity
 
and
 
XNLI
 
accuracy.
 
Expected
 
contributions
 
are:
 
(i)
 
a
 
multilingual
 
study
 
of
 
superword
 
tokenizers,
 
(ii)
 
a
 
fairness-aware
 
tokenizer
 
algorithm,
 
and
 
(iii)
 
a
